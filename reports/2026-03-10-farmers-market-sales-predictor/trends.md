## Executive Summary

The farmers market vendor tech space sits at the intersection of three growing markets: farmers market vendor tooling, cloud-based inventory management (8–14% CAGR), and AI-powered demand forecasting. The total addressable vendor population is roughly 150,000–430,000 active vendors across 8,600+ US markets. The industry itself generates an estimated $6.3B in annual revenue. Competitor funding is modest (Barn2Door: $19.5M total; Local Line: bootstrapped-adjacent), signaling the space is not yet crowded with well-capitalized players. The integration hook — Square POS — is the dominant POS platform at farmers markets, with a robust third-party API and App Marketplace. Seasonality is a real constraint: demand for a tool like this would spike sharply from March through October. Overall demand trajectory: **growing market**, with tailwinds from rising AI forecasting adoption among SMBs, and an underserved niche in perishable inventory prediction.

## 2. Growth Trajectory

### Farmers Market Industry

- Number of US farmers markets: **8,600–8,771** (USDA AMS; peaked 2019, growth flattened since 2016–2017 to below 1%/yr)
- Industry revenue: **$6.3 billion in 2025** (IBISWorld), growing at a modest **0.6% CAGR** over the prior 5 years
- Revenue expected to grow **1.8%** in 2025 alone, on higher average prices
- Vendor count: The Fruit & Vegetable Markets industry has **17,282 businesses** in the US, growing at a **7.3% CAGR** (2020–2025)
- SNAP redemptions at farmers markets surged **162%** since 2017, indicating growing market access programs
- 2023 Benchmark Survey: Vendors reported **17.38% mean increase in gross sales** YoY, and **10.65% increase in customer numbers** — strong organic growth at the vendor level even as market count plateaus

### Vendor Density

- Average vendors per seasonal market: **51** (USDA 2019 Managers Survey)
- At 8,600 markets × 51 vendors average = **~438,600 estimated active vendor slots** in the US
- Not all slots are unique vendors (some vendors operate at multiple markets), but this gives a ceiling for the addressable vendor count
- USDA 2020 local food data: **147,000 US farms** actively sold food through direct marketing channels

### Vendor Tech Adoption

- Less than 20% of farmers market payments are now made in cash (2025); the majority use card/contactless — meaning most vendors already have a POS device
- Square is the dominant POS among small/informal vendors due to low cost and no monthly fee
- Farm-specific platforms (Local Line, Barn2Door, Locally Grown) are gaining traction but serve a small minority

## 5. Seasonality

### Farmers Market Operating Season

- Most US farmers markets are **seasonal**, operating spring through fall
- Peak season: **June–August** (summer produce at peak; highest foot traffic)
- Secondary peak: **May and September** (early and late season)
- Year-round markets: primarily on the West Coast, Southeast, and Southwest
- Implication: A tool like MarketPrep would see a **sharp seasonal demand curve** — onboarding interest would peak in **March–April** (vendors preparing for the season), with peak usage June–August, and a retention challenge in November–February for most users

### Vendor Revenue Seasonality

- Experienced vendors at prime summer markets: **$1,500–$2,500/day in peak season**
- Off-peak or slower markets: **$200–$400/day**
- Weather is cited as the single biggest variable — bad weather at a market can drop sales to ~$1,000 or lower even at top markets
- This is a direct demand signal for the weather-integrated forecasting feature of MarketPrep

### Search Seasonality (Inferred)

- Searches for farmers market management tools, vendor applications, and booth fees would be expected to peak in **February–April** (pre-season planning) and tail off in **October–November**
- No direct Google Trends data was retrievable through web search; this should be verified at trends.google.com for terms: "farmers market vendor," "farmers market inventory," "Square POS farmers market"

### EBT / SNAP at Farmers Markets

- 250,000+ farmers market transactions/month made using EBT (2025 estimate)
- Growing food assistance usage at markets signals **broader vendor participation** and consistent foot traffic

## 7. The Core Problem: Waste from Poor Pack Decisions

- Farming generated **16.9 million tons of surplus produce** in 2024; 80%+ left unharvested or wasted
- **14% of food spoils before reaching retailers**, costing $400B/year globally (FAO)
- Overstocking at the vendor level stems directly from **inaccurate demand forecasting**
- Weather, market venue, season, and day-of-week are the primary demand variables — all modelable
- Vendors currently rely on intuition and manual experience to decide what to pack — no software specifically solves this for the farmers market context

| Dimension | Assessment |

| Seasonality | Highly seasonal; acquisition window is Feb–April each year |

**Overall demand verdict: Growing market with an underserved niche.** The macro conditions (inventory software growth, AI forecasting adoption, local food demand) are all pointing up. The specific problem (inventory prediction for farmers market vendors) has no funded, focused solution. This is an opportunity window, not a sure thing — the market is real but small enough that pricing and churn will be decisive factors.
